# Video delivery and RTCP repair research — 2026-10-28

Research and scratch experiments only. Halo source `6677086`; Scrypted source `50b8668ea8d36f281d5ab6d152cd47f3d45e57f5`. No product/server edits, no deployment, no camera/media capture. Root owns the combined engineering plan.

## What the real observation establishes

The reported 216-second stream remained owned/connected, but sent 997,960 video payload bytes and 2,193 RTP packets while the owner saw freezes. This is about **36.96 kbit/s average payload**, not proof that the selected 299 kbit/s ceiling was reached. Packet count is not frame count: one frame can need several packets, and retransmissions increment the same counters. Assuming this interval/counter pair covers the whole stream, unique delivered access units cannot exceed packet count: the upper bound is roughly 10.2 frames/s, slightly higher when startup is excluded. Actual source/output FPS, frame drops, loss, and client decoding are unmeasured. A healthy connection and successful local UDP send cannot prove smooth decoding.

## Findings and confidence

### V1. Confirmed cadence defect, conditional camera relevance

`media/transcode_worker.py:144–145` rejects a decoded frame when its source timestamp is less than one rounded selected period after the **last accepted** frame. This is a greedy interval filter, not a phase-preserving cadence limiter. `media/transcode.py:375–380` can also force adaptation after a single slightly short source period, even when its long-run rate matches the selection.

The actual isolated worker received 60 generated frames at selected 30 fps:

| Source timestamp steps at 90 kHz | Input frames | Output frames | Output step |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3000 | 60 | 60 | 3000 |
| alternating 2999 / 3001 | 60 | 30 | 6000 |

Only one tick (11 microseconds) of alternating quantization jitter halved the output rate. `cadence-probe.py` / `cadence-results.json` reproduce this without editing production or saving media. This does **not** establish that the Ring camera has these deltas.

The six-second long-GOP-source experiment (`long-source-gop-results.json`) additionally measured 2-second output IDRs in the control and 4-second IDRs in the halved-rate case. The encoder uses selected-rate frame counting (`gop_size=fps*2`, internal PTS counter at `1/fps`), while outgoing RTP keeps source PTS. Source-provided 2-second I frames can hide this stretch; `gop-results.json` records that control too. Sparse/slow input can therefore stretch the intended recovery interval and distort rate-control's time model.

**Plan:** use a bounded rational source-time phase for selection. Preserve small timestamp quantization jitter; discard genuinely over-rate input by phase, not a new full-period wait from each last accepted frame. Do not invent extra captured frames. Feed encoder PTS representing actual source time on a suitable rational time base. Explicitly force an output IDR no later than 2 seconds of admitted source time after the previous output IDR; verify NAL type 5 and repeated SPS/PPS, not just `is_keyframe`. Reset cadence/GOP state on capture/config epoch and validated discontinuity. Recheck the copy gate's instantaneous-rate heuristic so one tick of jitter does not force unnecessary encoding, while genuinely faster sources still adapt.

### V2. Confirmed dependency-recovery state weakness

`media/homekit_live.py:757–766` marks a stale frame, then clears `discarded_stale` **before** confirming a new IDR was delivered. It requests a keyframe and skips one P frame. If the adapter returns two fresh non-IDR outputs from one feed, the second is sent before any IDR. The bounded probe using the real `_send_video` state machine sent `freshP2` after dropping the stale reference and `freshP1`; see `stale-chain-results.json`. The adapter was synthetic; frequency on this Ring stream is unknown. Also, in copy mode `AccessUnitAdapter.request_keyframe` only enters local IDR waiting (`transcode.py:265–269`); it does not ask the established Ring receiver to send another IDR. The receiver currently sends startup PLIs only. A Home PLI can therefore wait for the next natural source IDR; this is a confirmed control-path limitation, not evidence that Home sent a PLI during the observed freeze.

**Plan:** a persistent `awaiting_decodable_idr` fence survives every dependent output and clears only after a complete current-epoch IDR with current parameter sets has been sent. Adaptation forces a local IDR; compatible copy gains a narrow, current-lease/capture-epoch source-keyframe callback to the existing Ring receiver, reusing its validated per-receiver PLI seam, or waits for a source IDR with a finite recovery budget. No second Ring connection is introduced. Never send another dependent frame merely because one P frame was skipped. Partial-AU transport failure must retain that recovery fence.

### V3. Confirmed budget/freshness weaknesses; not established as this freeze's cause

`homekit_live.py:799–834` computes the complete protected-RTP byte duration and permits up to `MAX_ACCESS_UNIT_SECONDS=1`. It checks source age before beginning the AU, but `_paced_send` does not apply that source receipt deadline to each video datagram. Thus an AU admitted just under the 500 ms age bound can finish later than that bound. `au-deadline-results.json` exercises the actual AU sender and pacer with a deterministic clock and a valid generated 3379-byte H.264 IDR: admitted at age490ms, six protected packets, last send at573ms, three packets after500ms. This is a confirmed delivery-bound weakness, not a live-camera timing measurement. A new AU that cannot fit the one-second wire budget returns false; an already-adapted AU then raises `media_saturated` and retires the entire view (`:790–796`). A transient oversized IDR need not be a permanent session failure.

The encoder's 299 kbit/s applies to encoded H.264, whereas the pacer meters protected RTP at 299 kbit/s. Headers, FU-A overhead, and retransmissions consume that same wire budget. For full-sized 1200-byte protected datagrams, even the 22-byte RTP/SRTP overhead implies payload capacity below 299 kbit/s; smaller fragmented frames cost more. This arithmetic mismatch can create backlog near the ceiling. It does not explain why the observed average was only 37 kbit/s by itself.

**Plan:** define `selected_video_bitrate`, `encoded_payload_budget`, `protected_RTP_budget`, and repair overhead explicitly. Keep the current conservative peer ceiling until its semantics are independently qualified. Reserve actual packetization overhead and bounded recovery headroom in the managed encoder budget. Pre-admit complete AUs against both byte budget and an explicit source-age/completion deadline. If an AU is temporarily infeasible, discard before first transmission, preserve session ownership, and enter dependency-safe IDR recovery. Adapt encoder peak/VBV/quality within the selected format so a replacement IDR can fit. Repeated infeasibility has a finite, explicit failure threshold; no infinite retry or old-media admission. Do not increase bitrate blindly or relax the 500 ms limit.

### V4. Confirmed observability gap, not evidence of a particular network fault

`rtp.py:126–168` retains only matching RR count, PLI, NACK sequence IDs, and BYE. RR fraction/cumulative loss, extended highest sequence, jitter, LSR and DLSR are discarded. `homekit_live.py:903–930` handles feedback but does not retain RR or repair outcomes. Packet/octet totals include retransmissions. Only START, first-frame, and terminal video metadata are logged; actual ongoing FPS/frame drops/IDR intervals are unavailable.

**Plan:** fixed-size counters/histograms for source AUs/deltas/receipt-gap bins; decoder/selected/output AUs; IDRs; pre/post-conversion stale drops; phase-rate drops; dependency skips; encode/wait/pacing latency; first/last-AU age; payload/protected bytes and unique/retransmitted packets separately. Add authenticated, own-SSRC RR fields and a bounded RTT estimator from a matching recent outbound SR's LSR/DLSR. Preserve signed cumulative-loss semantics and report stale/reordered/missing RR as unknown. Jitter units are the media RTP clock, not milliseconds until converted. Source Ring receive statistics and Home receiver statistics must remain separate. Poll/emit summaries at bounded frequency and expose only trusted local diagnostics, not a new public plugin schema.

## Comparison with primary implementations and protocols

- Pinned Scrypted classic sender [`camera-streaming-srtp-sender.ts`, lines 102–120, 159–180](https://github.com/koush/scrypted/blob/50b8668ea8d36f281d5ab6d152cd47f3d45e57f5/plugins/homekit/src/types/camera/camera-streaming-srtp-sender.ts#L102): repacketizes and sends immediately; no matching strict Halo AU wire pacer. It pairs the latest RTP timestamp with a local NTP timestamp for SR. Copying its larger socket buffer or immediate send behavior would hide queueing and can create bursts; this is a comparison, not a recommendation.
- Scrypted [`camera-streaming.ts`, lines 271–275, 340–350](https://github.com/koush/scrypted/blob/50b8668ea8d36f281d5ab6d152cd47f3d45e57f5/plugins/homekit/src/types/camera/camera-streaming.ts#L271) logs receiver packet loss and optionally forwards feedback upstream. Halo needs similarly visible evidence before deciding whether loss recovery, congestion, or codec scheduling is responsible.
- Scrypted optional video-transcode path [`camera-streaming-ffmpeg.ts`, lines 54–60](https://github.com/koush/scrypted/blob/50b8668ea8d36f281d5ab6d152cd47f3d45e57f5/plugins/homekit/src/types/camera/camera-streaming-ffmpeg.ts#L54) configures bitrate/maxrate/buffer and frame rate; default generally copies. No FFmpeg executable/backend change is proposed for Halo.
- [RFC 6184 section 5.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6184#section-5.1) defines H.264 RTP sampling/marker semantics; [section 8.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6184#section-8.5) discusses decoder refresh. Use complete access units and dependency-safe refresh; receiving UDP is not sufficient.
- [RFC 3550 section 6.4.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3550#section-6.4.1) supplies RR measurement fields and SR timing context; [section 6.4.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3550#section-6.4.4) explains their interpretation. These measurements can distinguish delivery loss and delay from source starvation.
- [RFC 4585 sections 6.2.1 and 6.3.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4585#section-6.2.1) define generic NACK and PLI; [section 7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4585#section-7) requires feedback/congestion consideration. A PLI is a decoder-refresh request, not permission for an unbounded keyframe burst.
- [FFmpeg public codec options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-codecs.html#Codec-Options) distinguish codec bitrate, maximum rate and VBV buffer; [libx264 options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-codecs.html#libx264_002c-libx264rgb) are the pinned-library controls already used by Halo. Encoder PTS and actual elapsed media time must agree for predictable output budgeting.
- [Homebridge public camera API](https://github.com/homebridge/HAP-NodeJS/blob/latest/src/lib/camera/RTPStreamManagement.ts#L1190) still exposes `max_bit_rate`, RTCP interval and MTU. [Homebridge issue 959](https://github.com/homebridge/HAP-NodeJS/issues/959) discusses the exact 299 value but was closed in July 2026 without resolving the proposed minimum/maximum reinterpretation. Treat it as unresolved evidence, **not authority to exceed the selected value**. Current public implementation decodes TLV type 3 as maximum bitrate.

## RTCP anti-abuse and lifetime constraints to preserve

Authenticate with the current per-track SRTP context before any statistics, liveness or repair authority. Retain exact current boot/profile/device/privacy/capture epoch checks. Keep structural bounds (16 KiB, 32 compound packets, 128 expanded NACK IDs), current admission limits and one-second/512-packet/1 MiB retransmission cache. Deduplicate NACKs; count missing/expired entries; use a bounded byte/time repair budget sharing the sender clock. PLI coalesces/rate-limits and never spawns concurrent encoders. Do not await a long repair burst inside the receive loop; enqueue only bounded current repairs so newer RR/BYE and policy revocation remain prompt. Use only matching media SSRC RR blocks; retain a small recent-SR ledger to validate LSR and derive RTT. Invalid/unrelated reports never become media authority or unbounded history. Logging/stat errors cannot stop media or expose addresses, keys, payloads, SSRC IDs, or raw exceptions.

## Proposed video slices and acceptance gates

1. **V0 — Measurement contract.** `rtp.py`, `homekit_live.py`, `transcode.py`, optional receiver stats seam in `ring_media.py`/`session.py`, trusted diagnostics status. Pure synthetic RR/LSR/DLSR tests including wrap, negative cumulative loss, wrong SSRC, stale/reordered reports and auth failures. Fixed counters and bounded log volume; no media-path outcome changes. Capture 5/15/30/long-run source/output FPS, arrival distributions, encode cost, recovery and RR baselines.
2. **V1 — Cadence and IDR correctness.** `transcode_worker.py`, `transcode.py`, `homekit_live.py`; new focused native cadence and dependency tests. Gates: 15/24/30 fps and fractional-rate inputs, ±one-tick jitter, slower/faster source, long source GOP, source timestamp wrap/discontinuity. No artificial 50% decimation; no faster output than selection; source-time IDR deadline ≤2 s; output IDR proof via native decode/NAL5; multi-output stale-chain test never emits a P frame before recovery IDR. Compatible compressed-copy bytes stay exact.
3. **V2 — Fresh complete-AU delivery and bounded recovery.** `homekit_live.py`, encoder configuration in worker, optional small private AU queue helper. Gate isolated local UDP/SRTP + independent decode with moving/high-entropy IDRs near299k, bursts and injected scheduling stalls. At most two bounded AUs if concurrency is introduced; no stale delivery, unbounded queues, leaked encoder/capture claims, session death on one infeasible AU, or policy/capture-epoch escape. Measure actual steady FPS and queue/first-to-last-packet latency under simultaneous native audio. Test same-wire-budget loss recovery without starving new media.
4. **V3 — Qualified feedback recovery and real-device confirmation.** `rtp.py`/`homekit_live.py`, exact bounded retransmit scheduling; no guessed automatic bitrate increase. Deterministic loss/reorder/NACK/PLI/RR simulator with finite repair latency, retransmission hit/miss accounting and loss storm caps. Long synthetic run plus Home-controlled LAN/remote and reconfigure cases after approval. At least 5-minute native synthetic motion/no-motion runs and actual owner observation with source/output/receiver counters, no repeating freeze and no audio/epoch/cleanup regression. Retain a known-good release and pairing data; HKSV remains off.

The 216-second live freeze is not yet assigned to V1, V2, packet loss, source bursts or Apple decode behavior. Those are differentiated by V0 measurements and the targeted gates above; the confirmed conditional defects can be repaired without pretending they alone explain the current camera.


## Mapping into Root's seven-slice plan

- Root1 bounded telemetry: V0 above. Source receiver `getStats` sampling is a bounded owner task, not a global monkeypatch. Keep RR metrics separate from source media admission.
- Root2 audio recovery/pacer and Root3 legacy lock isolation are owned by other researchers. Video measurements must include their simultaneous load.
- Root4 cadence/GOP: V1's native phase/PTS and 2-second source-time IDR work.
- **Root5 video paced/dependency recovery/feedback:** persistent IDR-required state, complete-AU pre-admission/deadline, nonterminal transient oversize recovery, explicit encoder-vs-wire/repair budgets, current-epoch source PLI callback for copy, bounded nonblocking NACK repair scheduling. Target files: `media/homekit_live.py`, `media/rtp.py`, `media/transcode.py`, `media/transcode_worker.py`; narrowly additive `homekit/controller.py`, `homekit/service_backend.py`, `media/session.py`/`ring_media.py` only for the established-receiver keyframe callback. Do not modify bulk IPC, HKSV/recording buffers, public plugin schemas, HAP pairing, or source ownership rules.
- Root6 quality/A-V/reconfigure: measure source SR availability/fallback, per-track presentation deltas, source/output FPS and actual bitrate after preceding repairs. Qualify rather than guess a higher rate from the unresolved299 discussion; preserve UDP ports, sender SSRC, SRTP index, and ownership through reconfigure. Explicit selection changes can reset local codec/cadence state without resetting crypto state.
- Root7 package/pilot: unchanged reproducible offline release workflow, current installed native probes plus longer variable-cadence/motion/loss runs, public HAP lifecycle regressions, then separately authorized real-device confirmation. Research receipts do not certify live behavior.

Root5 gate specifics: valid generated IDR admitted at490ms must either be wholly pre-rejected or complete within the chosen receipt bound, never silently straddle it; one unsendable AU must not tear down an otherwise valid view; dependent AUs remain fenced until a complete current-epoch IDR; authenticated NACK hit/miss/expired/duplicate/wrap cases remain bounded and share the byte clock; repeated loss cannot starve new media or block BYE/privacy retirement; source-PLI callbacks are coalesced, bounded and invalidated on source epoch/boot changes; source-parameter changes cannot reuse stale SPS/PPS. No packet/frame payloads or key material are saved by these gates.
